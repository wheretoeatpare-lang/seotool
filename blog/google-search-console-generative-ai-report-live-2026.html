Google's GSC AI Report Just Went Live for (Almost) Everyone — Here's How to Actually Use It

📅 Aug 15, 2026 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ RankSorcery Team
📊

⚡ TL;DR

  • Google's Generative AI Performance report in Search Console expanded to way more properties this week — check your account, it's probably there now, tucked under an expandable tab in Performance.
  • It shows impressions, pages, countries, devices, and date trends for how often you show up in AI Overviews and AI Mode — but still zero click or query data.
  • Google will only show the report if your site has "enough impressions" in generative AI features, so a blank report is itself a data point.
  • Use it to find pages that get cited a lot but drive no visible traffic, then cross-reference with your regular Performance report and server logs to build the picture Google won't hand you.

I checked six client Search Console accounts this week that had never shown a peep of AI data before. All six now have a "Generative AI features" tab sitting quietly under the regular Performance report. No email from Google. No changelog entry saying "hey, we just turned this on for you." It just showed up, the way most Google features do — silently, and only if you happen to go looking.

This is the report Google's been trickling out since earlier this year, and as of the last week or so it's expanded to a lot more sites than before. It's not a full 100% rollout — John Mueller confirmed on Reddit that it's "not yet every domain" — but if you haven't looked at your GSC account in a couple weeks, go check right now. There's a decent chance it's there.

What's Actually In the Report

Open Search Console, go to Performance, and look for a second expandable section below the main "Search results" tab. If your site qualifies, you'll see "Generative AI features" broken out with its own filters. The data included is:

  • Impressions — how often a URL from your site showed up inside an AI Overview, AI Mode response, or Discover's generative surfaces.
  • Pages — which specific URLs are getting picked up, ranked by impression volume.
  • Countries — geographic breakdown of where those impressions are happening.
  • Devices — desktop vs. mobile split (for Search results specifically).
  • Dates — hourly, daily, weekly, and monthly trend lines, so you can actually watch a spike happen.

Notice what's missing from that list: clicks, and queries. You get to see that Google is showing your content to people inside its AI answers. You don't get to see what they searched for to trigger it, and you don't get any click-through data tied to those impressions. It's visibility data with the two most useful columns surgically removed.

💡 Quick Take

If the report isn't showing up for your site at all, don't panic — Google explicitly says it withholds the report when a site "hasn't received enough impressions in generative AI features." A missing report is a signal too: it probably means AI surfaces aren't picking you up much yet, which is its own useful (if depressing) data point.

Why Google Left the Query and Click Data Out

I don't think this is an accident or a "we'll add it later" situation. Google has been extremely cagey about exposing anything that lets publishers build a clean before/after picture of AI Overviews' impact on their traffic. Impressions-only data lets Google say "look, we're being transparent, here's a whole new report" while still controlling the narrative around whether AI features are actually costing sites clicks.

The honest answer, based on everything site owners have been reporting all year, is that impressions in generative AI features are going up while total clicks to many sites are going down. Google doesn't want a report that makes that math easy to do in one screen. So you get impressions in one tab and clicks in another, and you're left to do the join yourself.

The workaround: build the comparison manually

Here's what's actually useful about having this data, even incomplete:

  • Export your regular Search results Performance data (clicks, impressions, CTR, position) for your top 200-300 URLs over the same date range.
  • Export the Generative AI features data for the same URLs and date range.
  • Join them by URL in a spreadsheet. Look for pages where AI impressions are climbing while regular search clicks are flat or falling. That's your ghost traffic — pages doing work for Google's AI answers without sending you anything back.

What To Actually Do With This Report

Don't just admire the chart. Here's how I'd use it on a real site starting today.

1. Find your AI-favorite pages

Sort the Pages tab by impressions. These are the URLs Google's models keep reaching for when they need supporting evidence. Usually it's a mix of definitional content, comparison pages, and anything with clear, extractable facts — stats, steps, specific numbers. Once you know which pages get picked, you can double down: tighten the exact passage that's probably being lifted, add clearer attribution cues (author name, publish date, source citations), and make sure the surrounding content still gives humans a reason to click through instead of just reading the AI's paraphrase.

2. Watch the trend line, not the snapshot

A single week of data tells you almost nothing. What matters is whether your AI impressions are trending up or down after a content update, after a core update, or after you restructure a page. Bookmark this report and check it monthly alongside your regular rankings review.

3. Cross-reference with server logs if you can

If you have access to raw server logs, filter for Google-Extended and Googlebot hits on the pages showing high AI impressions. You'll get a much better sense of crawl frequency tied to AI feature eligibility than GSC alone will ever give you.

4. Use country and device splits to catch localization gaps

If a page is getting heavy AI impressions in one country but you have zero localized content or currency/measurement adjustments for that market, that's a quick, high-leverage fix. AI features are pulling your English-language content into markets you might not have been actively targeting.

The Bigger Pattern Here

This rollout fits a pattern I've been seeing all year: Google adds just enough visibility into AI search performance to make it look like they're being transparent, while carefully withholding the exact data points (queries, clicks, revenue attribution) that would let publishers prove harm or negotiate leverage. Search Console's AI Impressions groundwork started earlier this year, expanded a few weeks ago, and is expanding again now. Each step adds more rows and columns but never adds the two that would actually let you calculate ROI.

That doesn't mean the report is useless — it's genuinely useful for prioritization and trend-spotting. It just means you shouldn't treat "impressions in generative AI features" as a proxy for "value I'm getting from AI search." Impressions without clicks is exposure, not traffic. Track it, use it to guide content decisions, but don't let it replace the metrics that actually pay your bills.

What This Means If You're Running an Agency or Managing Multiple Sites

If you manage more than a handful of properties, this rollout is a good excuse to build a standing checklist. Pull the Generative AI features report for every client account once a month, not just the ones you suspect are getting hit hardest. I've been surprised more than once by a small local services site with almost no backlink profile showing meaningful AI impressions on a single well-structured FAQ page, while a much bigger content site with hundreds of blog posts barely registers.

The pattern that's emerging is that specificity beats scale here. Pages that answer one clear question precisely — with a direct number, a defined term, a short list of steps — get pulled into AI answers more often than long, sprawling posts that cover a topic broadly but never commit to a crisp, quotable answer. If you're auditing client sites for AI visibility, that's the first thing to check: does this page actually contain a sentence Google's model could lift wholesale and be confident it's correct?

Build a simple tracking sheet — client name, top 5 AI-impression pages, month-over-month trend, and a column for "action taken." It doesn't need to be fancy. What matters is that you're actually looking at this report on a schedule instead of only checking it when a client asks why their traffic dropped.

Bottom Line

Go check your Search Console account today — there's a real chance this report is now live on your property even if it wasn't a few weeks ago. Pull the Pages tab, sort by impressions, and start building your own manual comparison against regular click data. Don't wait for Google to hand you the full picture. They've made it pretty clear they're not going to.
